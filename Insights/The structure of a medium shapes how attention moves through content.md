# The structure of a medium shapes how attention moves through content

## Claim

Books generally guide readers through content linearly, while internet content permits frequent skipping and context switching; this structural difference can produce a chain of downstream effects.

## Original Observation

Source capture: [[Stolen Focus#^capture-20260829-stolen-focus-02]]

![[Stolen Focus#^capture-20260829-stolen-focus-02]]

## Why It Matters

> [!note] Agent-organized interpretation
> This treats the navigation structure of a medium as part of the reading experience, rather than treating attention as independent of how content is presented.

## Evidence / Examples

- The reflection contrasts the generally linear progression of reading a book with the ability to skip among internet content.
- Context switching is identified as a characteristic of the latter reading mode.

## Tensions / Counterexamples

> [!note] Agent-suggested qualification
> Books can be read selectively, and some internet content can be followed sequentially, so the distinction may describe a tendency rather than an absolute boundary.
