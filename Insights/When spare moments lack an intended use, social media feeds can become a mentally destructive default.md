# When spare moments lack an intended use, social media feeds can become a mentally destructive default

## Claim

Not knowing how to use brief periods of spare time makes social-media feeds appear to be a reasonable solution, even when relying on them is mentally destructive; those periods therefore need more deliberate alternatives.

## Original Observation

Source capture: [[Stolen Focus#^capture-20260830-stolen-focus-03]]

![[Stolen Focus#^capture-20260830-stolen-focus-03]]

## Why It Matters

> [!note] Agent-organized interpretation
> This locates part of the problem before a feed is opened: when a brief uncommitted period has no intended use, the feed can become the easiest available default.

## Evidence / Examples

- The reflection identifies uncertainty about how to use short periods of spare time.
- Social media is described as offering a solution that looks reasonable while being mentally destructive.

## Tensions / Counterexamples

> [!note] Agent-suggested qualification
> Making good use of spare time need not mean making it productive; deliberate rest or doing nothing may also be worthwhile alternatives to a feed.

- The specific mental effects described as destructive have not yet been captured.
- Social-media use may itself be deliberate; the concern here is relying on the feed as the default response.
